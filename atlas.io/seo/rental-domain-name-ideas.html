Six rules for a name that sticks

A domain customers can remember after one call is worth more than a clever one they forget. Lead with these.

Keep it short

Two or three words at most. A short name is easier to say on the phone, print on a sign and remember after one glance.

Say what you rent

Put your category in the name — boats, bounce houses, cameras, cars. Customers and search engines both reward a name that is obvious.

Add your city

Local renters search by place. A city or region in the name wins nearby customers and sidesteps a crowded national .com.

Prefer a .com

Still the most trusted and the easiest to recall. If it is taken, a specific ending like .rentals or your city code can be a strong second choice.

Skip hyphens and numbers

“Is that spelled with a dash, or the number four?” Every character you have to explain on the phone is a booking you might lose.

Say it out loud

Read the name to someone and have them type it back. If they get it right the first time, it will survive the radio ad and the word-of-mouth referral.
